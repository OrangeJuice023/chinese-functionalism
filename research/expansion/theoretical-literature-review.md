# Theoretical Literature Review (Expansion)

## A. Functionalism and Regional Integration
Mitrany (1943, 1948) proposed function-specific agencies, technical self-determination, and pooling of sovereignty for specific tasks. He was not apolitical; his work drew on pluralist theory and aimed at undermining nationalism. Neofunctionalism (Haas 1958, 1964) added spillover mechanisms: functional, political, cultivated, plus spillback. Application outside Europe is contested; European experience may not transfer.

## B. Liberal Intergovernmentalism
Moravcsik (1998, 2005) explains integration via state preferences, interstate bargaining, and institutional delegation. It may better explain China-ASEAN cooperation, which is driven by state interests and bargaining rather than automatic spillover. Functional cooperation may be an outcome of state choice, not a self-reinforcing process.

## C. Economic Statecraft
Economic instruments (trade, investment, infrastructure finance, market access, coercion) serve strategic objectives. Overlaps with proposed framework in sectoral cooperation; differs in explicit focus on power and leverage. May better explain China's behavior than functionalism.

## D. Complex Interdependence
Multiple channels and reduced military salience. In asymmetric relationships, interdependence may create vulnerabilities and leverage rather than reduce conflict. Relevant to China-ASEAN.

## E. Chinese IR Scholarship
Relational theory (Qin), moral realism (Yan), Tianxia (Zhao), social symbiosis (Ren). These are civilizational/relational/moral, not functionalist. Do not classify as functionalist. Chinese School debate reflects identity and status quest (He 2015).

## Comparative Assessment
- Classical functionalism: strong on sectoral cooperation, weak on power and politics.
- Neofunctionalism: strong on integration dynamics, weak on transferability and falsifiability.
- Liberal intergovernmentalism: strong on state preferences and bargaining, weaker on long-term institutional effects.
- Economic statecraft: strong on power and leverage, weaker on institutional cooperation.
- Complex interdependence: strong on multiple channels, weaker on asymmetry.
- Chinese IR theories: strong on civilizational framing, weak on empirical testability.
- Proposed Chinese Functionalism: potentially synthesizes functional cooperation with power asymmetry, but distinctiveness unproven.

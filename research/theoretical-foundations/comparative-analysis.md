# Theoretical Comparative Analysis

## Classical Functionalism (Mitrany)
- **Core claim:** Peace through function-specific agencies; pooling sovereignty needed for specific tasks; technical self-determination.
- **Mechanism:** Functional cooperation creates vested interests and cross-border bonds that erode nationalism.
- **Limitations:** Assumes technical tasks can be separated from politics; optimistic about de-politicization; weak on power asymmetry.

## Neofunctionalism (Haas, Schmitter)
- **Core claim:** Integration spillover: sectoral cooperation generates political integration via interest groups and supranational entrepreneurs.
- **Mechanism:** Technical/functional spillover, political spillover, cultivated spillover.
- **Limitations:** Over-predicted automaticity; struggled with stagnation and spillback; criticized as unfalsifiable and elite-focused.

## Liberal Institutionalism
- **Core claim:** Institutions reduce transaction costs, provide information, and facilitate cooperation under anarchy.
- **Limitations:** Assumes shared interests; less attention to power asymmetry and distributional conflict.

## Constructivism
- **Core claim:** Norms, identities, and socialization shape state interests and behavior.
- **Limitations:** Harder to falsify; may underweight material power.

## Complex Interdependence
- **Core claim:** Multiple channels of contact; absence of hierarchy among issues; minor role of military force.
- **Limitations:** Understates coercion and security competition.

## Chinese IR Theories (Moral Realism, Relational Theory, Tianxia, Symbiosis)
- **Core claims:** Moral/relational/hierarchical or symbiotic conceptions of order; challenge Western state-centric paradigms.
- **Limitations:** Often state-centric and normatively loaded; limited empirical testing; risk of justifying state interests.

## Implications for Chinese Functionalism
- Functional cooperation is observable in China's practice (trade, infrastructure, connectivity, institutions).
- Whether this constitutes a "functionalist" doctrine or pragmatic statecraft is an open question.
- Power asymmetry and strategic interests must be central, not assumed away.
- The framework must be falsifiable: specify scope conditions and disconfirming evidence.

# Dossier 01 — Theoretical Implications

The new evidence supports a descriptive claim: China and Southeast Asian states participate in extensive sectoral and institutional cooperation under unequal conditions, with uneven outcomes.

- Classical functionalism: evidence of sectoral cooperation and interdependence, but state primacy and asymmetry challenge anti-statist assumptions.
- Neofunctionalism: limited evidence of automatic spillover; spillback and resistance present.
- Economic statecraft: strong fit for strategic use of trade, loans, and infrastructure.
- Liberal intergovernmentalism: strong fit for state bargaining and institutional negotiation.
- Complex interdependence: multiple channels present, but asymmetry creates vulnerabilities.
- Smaller-state agency: evidence of negotiation, resistance, and redirection, varying by capacity.

Formulation B (analytical adaptation) remains the provisional working direction; Formulation A (modified functionalism) remains competing. No distinctive causal mechanism established beyond existing theories.

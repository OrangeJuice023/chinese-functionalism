# Dossier 02 — Theoretical Implications

New evidence strengthens the empirical case for studying functional cooperation under asymmetry but does not establish a distinct theory.

- Classical functionalism: sectoral cooperation and interdependence present, but state primacy and asymmetry challenge anti-statist assumptions.
- Neofunctionalism: limited automatic spillover; institutional cooperation (DEFA, APG) is intergovernmental, not supranational.
- Economic statecraft: strong fit for strategic use of investment, technology, and supply chains.
- Global value-chain theory: explains foreign-owned exports, upstream dependence, and value capture gaps.
- Developmental-state approaches: explain Indonesia's downstreaming, Malaysia's industrial strategy.
- Liberal intergovernmentalism: explains ASEAN institutional negotiation.
- Complex interdependence: multiple channels present, but asymmetry creates vulnerabilities.

Formulation B remains provisional; distinctiveness unproven. The six-question analytical lens (functional need, institutional form, value distribution, asymmetry/control, agency, institutional consequences) offers a useful comparative checklist.

# Framework Critique — Chinese Functionalism

## Competing Formulations

### Formulation A: Modified Functionalism
Chinese Functionalism as a modification of classical functionalism: functional cooperation under state primacy, where the state remains the primary strategic actor and functional agencies serve state interests. Strengths: retains functionalist vocabulary; acknowledges power. Weaknesses: may be indistinguishable from economic statecraft; risks diluting functionalism beyond recognition.

### Formulation B: Analytical Adaptation
Chinese Functionalism as an analytical lens describing patterns of sectoral cooperation without claiming a doctrine: a heuristic for identifying functional cooperation, interdependence, and strategic use. Strengths: modest, falsifiable, avoids overclaiming. Weaknesses: may add little beyond existing concepts like economic statecraft or complex interdependence.

## Critical Questions

1. Can functionalism remain functionalist if the state remains primary strategic actor? Classical functionalism was anti-statist; retaining state primacy may make the framework something other than functionalism.
2. What is distinctively Chinese? Possibly the scale of state-led development finance, BRI, and civilizational framing. But these may be better explained by developmental state or hegemonic order theories.
3. Descriptive vs explanatory vs prescriptive: current framework mixes all three. Must be clarified.
4. Causal mechanisms: sectoral cooperation -> interdependence -> institutional habits -> ? The chain is speculative.
5. Necessary/sufficient conditions: not yet specified.
6. Falsification: framework must specify disconfirming evidence (purely extractive cooperation, systematic resistance, no spillover).
7. Coercion and failure: framework currently underaddresses these.

## Recommendation
Treat Formulation B as the working default for Draft v0.2, with Formulation A as a competing hypothesis to test. Do not select a preferred formulation without further evidence.

# Empirical Evidence Audit — China-ASEAN

## Areas

### Trade and Investment
- Evidence of functional cooperation: ACFTA, RCEP, growing trade volumes.
- Strategic interests: market access, supply chain integration.
- Power asymmetry: China's larger market; ASEAN's dependence on Chinese imports.
- Mutual benefit: cheaper goods, export markets.
- Resistance/failure: trade imbalances, non-tariff barriers.
- Alternative explanations: comparative advantage, global value chains.

### Infrastructure Connectivity
- Evidence: China-Laos Railway, Jakarta-Bandung HSR, BRI projects.
- Strategic interests: regional influence, supply chain control.
- Asymmetry: debt, financing terms, contractor dominance.
- Mutual benefit: improved logistics, connectivity.
- Resistance/failure: debt concerns, project delays, local opposition.
- Alternative explanations: developmental state, infrastructure gap filling.

### Digital and Technological Cooperation
- Evidence: ASEAN-China digital economy initiatives, 5G, e-commerce.
- Strategic interests: technology standards, data governance.
- Asymmetry: Chinese tech firms' dominance.
- Mutual benefit: digital trade, capacity building.
- Resistance: cybersecurity, data sovereignty concerns.
- Alternative: technology diffusion, market expansion.

### Energy and Environmental Cooperation
- Evidence: energy projects, green economy commitments in ACFTA 3.0.
- Strategic interests: energy security, green transition leadership.
- Asymmetry: financing and technology gaps.
- Mutual benefit: energy access, climate goals.
- Resistance: environmental concerns, project disputes.
- Alternative: energy security, climate governance.

### Institutional Cooperation
- Evidence: ASEAN+1, summits, POA 2026-2030.
- Strategic interests: institutional influence, norm setting.
- Asymmetry: China's agenda-setting power.
- Mutual benefit: dialogue, conflict management.
- Resistance: ASEAN centrality concerns.
- Alternative: institutionalism, diplomatic normalization.

### Maritime Cooperation and Disputes
- Evidence: maritime cooperation mechanisms, joint patrols.
- Strategic interests: territorial claims, sea lane control.
- Asymmetry: China's naval/coast guard dominance.
- Mutual benefit: fisheries management, safety.
- Resistance: South China Sea disputes, Philippine resistance.
- Alternative: security dilemma, sovereignty competition.

### Development Finance
- Evidence: ACCF, AIIB, bilateral loans.
- Strategic interests: influence, market creation.
- Asymmetry: debtor-creditor relations.
- Mutual benefit: infrastructure financing.
- Resistance: debt sustainability concerns.
- Alternative: development finance, economic statecraft.

### Smaller-State Autonomy and Hedging
- Evidence: ASEAN hedging, Philippine balancing.
- Strategic interests: preserve autonomy.
- Asymmetry: structural dependence.
- Mutual benefit: diversified partnerships.
- Resistance: pushback on specific projects.
- Alternative: hedging theory, small-state strategy.

## Key Observation
Existence of agreements does not prove cooperation succeeded. Need outcome-level evidence.

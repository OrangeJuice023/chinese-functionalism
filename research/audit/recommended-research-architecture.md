# Recommended Research Architecture

## Sharper Central Research Question
Does Chinese Functionalism, understood as a provisional analytical framework emphasizing sectoral cooperation under conditions of power asymmetry, explain patterns of China-ASEAN engagement that existing theories (liberal institutionalism, economic statecraft, complex interdependence) cannot adequately capture?

## Defensible Central Argument (Competing)
- Argument A: Chinese Functionalism adds value by centering power asymmetry and smaller-state agency within a functionalist vocabulary.
- Argument B: Existing frameworks (economic statecraft, complex interdependence) suffice; Chinese Functionalism is a relabeling.

## Clearer Definition (Provisional)
Chinese Functionalism is a proposed analytical framework for examining how states, particularly China, use sectoral and institutional cooperation to pursue strategic and welfare objectives under conditions of power asymmetry, while accounting for smaller-state responses and the limits of functionalist optimism.

## Conceptual Mechanism Description
Sectoral cooperation -> interdependence and institutional habits -> potential political effects, conditioned by power asymmetry, strategic interests, and smaller-state resistance; with possible spillback.

## Revised Observable Indicators
- Depth and scope of sectoral agreements.
- Institutional participation and leadership.
- Trade/investment/infrastructure flows.
- Smaller-state policy responses (hedging, resistance, exit).
- Incidence of spillback or failed cooperation.

## Scope Conditions
- Economic and technical domains primarily.
- Asymmetric power relations.
- Presence of institutional frameworks.

## Limitations and Falsification
- Falsified if cooperation is purely extractive, if smaller states systematically exit, or if no interdependence results.
- Limited in security domains.
- Risk of conflating statecraft with functionalism.

## Recommended Book Structure (Provisional)
1. Introduction and Problem
2. Classical Functionalism and Neofunctionalism
3. Chinese IR Scholarship and Alternative Frameworks
4. China-ASEAN Functional Cooperation: Evidence
5. Power Asymmetry and Smaller-State Responses
6. Chinese Functionalism: Framework Development and Critique
7. Philippine Case Study
8. Implications, Limitations, Future Research

# CONCEPT NOTE FOR COLLABORATORS

**Working Title:** Chinese Functionalism: Sectoral Cooperation, Power Asymmetry, and Regional Order in Southeast Asia

**Status:** PRELIMINARY WORKING DRAFT FOR COLLABORATOR REVIEW

## Background and Rationale

China's international engagement has expanded across trade, infrastructure, technology, and institutional cooperation. The theoretical meaning of these developments remains contested. This project investigates whether the concept of Chinese Functionalism can serve as a useful analytical framework.

## Central Research Problem

Does China's use of sectoral and institutional cooperation under conditions of power asymmetry constitute a functionalist pattern that existing theories cannot adequately explain?

## Research Question

Can Chinese Functionalism, understood as a provisional analytical framework, explain patterns of China-ASEAN engagement that liberal institutionalism, economic statecraft, and complex interdependence cannot capture?

## Proposed Conceptual Definition

Chinese Functionalism is a proposed analytical framework for examining how states, particularly China, use sectoral and institutional cooperation to pursue strategic and welfare objectives under conditions of power asymmetry, while accounting for smaller-state responses and the limits of functionalist optimism.

## Competing Theoretical Interpretations

Formulation A (modified functionalism): functional cooperation under state primacy. Formulation B (analytical adaptation): heuristic for identifying functional cooperation patterns. Established alternatives include liberal intergovernmentalism, economic statecraft, complex interdependence, and Chinese IR theories.

## Preliminary Central Argument

Functional cooperation between China and Southeast Asian states is extensive and documented, but it coexists with power asymmetry, strategic competition, and smaller-state resistance. Whether this pattern constitutes a distinct functionalist framework remains unproven. The project remains open to the possibility that established theories explain the evidence more convincingly.

## Empirical Scope

China-ASEAN relations, with focus on ACFTA, infrastructure projects, digital cooperation, and the Philippine case.

## Significance

Potentially offers a framework combining functional cooperation with power asymmetry and smaller-state agency. May also demonstrate that existing theories suffice.

## Relevance to Southeast Asia and the Philippines

Smaller states navigate cooperation and competition simultaneously. The Philippine case illustrates economic engagement constrained by maritime disputes.

## Proposed Book Structure

Part I: Understanding Functionalism. Part II: China and Functional Cooperation. Part III: Developing Chinese Functionalism. Part IV: The Philippine Perspective. Part V: Implications.

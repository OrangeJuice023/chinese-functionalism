# CONCEPT NOTE FOR COLLABORATORS v0.3

**Working Title:** Chinese Functionalism: Sectoral Cooperation, Power Asymmetry, and Regional Order in Southeast Asia

**Status:** PRELIMINARY WORKING MANUSCRIPT FOR ACADEMIC COLLABORATOR REVIEW

## Background and Intellectual Rationale

China's international engagement has expanded across trade, infrastructure, technology, and institutional cooperation. The theoretical meaning of these developments remains contested. Existing theories offer partial explanations, but none centers the combination of functional cooperation and power asymmetry that characterizes China's regional engagement.

This project investigates whether the concept of Chinese Functionalism can serve as a useful analytical framework. The concept is provisional and may be revised or rejected based on evidence.

## The Problem the Book Investigates

The central problem is whether China's use of sectoral and institutional cooperation under conditions of power asymmetry constitutes a functionalist pattern that existing theories cannot adequately explain. The existence of extensive cooperation is documented. What remains unclear is whether this cooperation generates the kind of interdependence and institutional dynamics that functionalist theory predicts, or whether it is better explained by state preferences, bargaining, and strategic calculation.

## Central Research Question

Can Chinese Functionalism, understood as a provisional analytical framework, explain patterns of China-ASEAN engagement that liberal institutionalism, economic statecraft, and complex interdependence cannot capture?

## Why This Project Matters

The project addresses a gap between the extensive documentation of China's functional cooperation and the limited theoretical frameworks available to interpret it. If functional cooperation under asymmetry produces distinct dynamics, identifying them could contribute to international relations scholarship. If established theories suffice, that finding is equally valuable.

## Proposed Definition of Chinese Functionalism

Chinese Functionalism is a proposed analytical framework for examining how states, particularly China, use sectoral and institutional cooperation to pursue strategic and welfare objectives under conditions of power asymmetry, while accounting for smaller-state responses and the limits of functionalist optimism.

## Formulation A and Formulation B

Formulation A, modified functionalism, treats Chinese Functionalism as a modification of classical functionalism under state primacy. It retains functionalist vocabulary but risks diluting the concept beyond recognition.

Formulation B, analytical adaptation, treats Chinese Functionalism as a heuristic for identifying functional cooperation patterns under asymmetry. It is more modest and falsifiable but may add little beyond existing concepts.

Neither formulation has been conclusively established. Formulation B is the provisional working direction.

## Competing Theoretical Explanations

The framework must be compared against classical functionalism, neofunctionalism, liberal institutionalism, liberal intergovernmentalism, economic statecraft, complex interdependence, strategic regionalism, and constructivism. Each offers distinct insights. The project remains open to the possibility that established theories explain the evidence more convincingly.

## Preliminary Central Argument

Functional cooperation between China and Southeast Asian states is extensive and documented, but it coexists with power asymmetry, strategic competition, and smaller-state resistance. Whether this pattern constitutes a distinct functionalist framework remains unproven. The project investigates this question rather than assuming an affirmative answer.

## Empirical Scope and Case Selection

The empirical focus is China-ASEAN relations, with attention to ACFTA, infrastructure projects, digital cooperation, and the Philippine case. Cases are treated as preliminary illustrations, not fully validated empirical studies. Official agreements are distinguished from implementation outcomes.

## Proposed Research Methodology

The project uses metadata-verified academic and official sources, with full-text review pending. Evidence is distinguished as verified fact, official claim, scholarly argument, project interpretation, or hypothesis. Negative cases and competing explanations are actively sought.

## Relevance to International Relations Scholarship

The project contributes to debates on functionalism, regional integration, economic statecraft, and Chinese IR theory. It may offer a framework combining functional cooperation with power asymmetry, or it may demonstrate that existing theories suffice.

## Relevance to Southeast Asia and the Philippines

Smaller states navigate cooperation and competition simultaneously. The Philippine case illustrates economic engagement constrained by maritime disputes, showing that functional cooperation does not eliminate strategic competition.

## Proposed Book Architecture

Part I: Understanding Functionalism. Part II: China and Functional Cooperation. Part III: Developing Chinese Functionalism. Part IV: The Philippine Perspective. Part V: Implications.

## Theoretical and Empirical Limitations

Full-text reviews are incomplete. Quantitative data are preliminary. The framework is untested against systematic evidence. Causal mechanisms remain speculative.

## 2. Central Subject

**CHINESE FUNCTIONALISM**

The project aims to investigate, conceptualize, critically examine, and potentially develop Chinese Functionalism as an analytical framework concerning China's international relations, regional cooperation, strategic interests, and engagement with Southeast Asia.

[PROVISIONAL — REQUIRES HUMAN REVIEW] The exact definition, scope, and theoretical boundaries of "Chinese Functionalism" are not yet finalized. They must emerge through research and collaboration.

## 3. Preliminary Research Objectives

- Investigate the theoretical foundations of functionalism and related international relations approaches.
- Assess the applicability of functionalist perspectives to China's international engagement.
- Analyze China's use of economic, institutional, political, technological, and other forms of functional cooperation.
- Examine regional cooperation and interdependence in Southeast Asia.
- Study ASEAN, regional institutions, and regional order.
- Analyze China's strategic interests and influence.
- Consider the experiences and policy choices of smaller states, particularly the Philippines.
- Explore the relationship between cooperation, competition, power asymmetry, and regional stability.
- Identify the limitations, contradictions, and possible criticisms of the framework.

## 4. Research Questions Requiring Investigation

- What is functionalism in international relations theory?
- How does neofunctionalism differ from classical functionalism?
- To what extent is Chinese Functionalism an existing concept vs. a proposed framework?
- How does China use functional cooperation as a strategic tool?
- What is the relationship between functional cooperation and power asymmetry?
- How do smaller states (e.g., Philippines) navigate China's functional engagement?
- What are the limitations of applying functionalist theory to China's behavior?
- What counterarguments challenge the framework?

## 5. Theoretical Foundations to Examine

- Functionalism (David Mitrany)
- Neofunctionalism (Ernst Haas, Philippe Schmitter)
- Regional integration theory
- Institutionalism (liberal, historical, constructivist)
- Power asymmetry and small-state agency
- Regional order and hegemony

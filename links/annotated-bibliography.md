## SRC-0001 — Mitrany, David (1943). A Working Peace System
- **Citation:** Mitrany, D. (1943). *A Working peace system: An argument for the functional development of international organization*. Royal Institute of International Affairs.
- **Central Argument:** International peace should be pursued through function-specific agencies rather than comprehensive political federation; functional arrangements pool sovereignty needed for specific tasks.
- **Theoretical Contribution:** Originates the functional approach to international organization; emphasizes technical self-determination and autonomous functional development.
- **Supporting Evidence:** Analysis of interwar organization failures; examples of sectoral cooperation.
- **Limitations:** Optimistic about de-politicization of technical tasks; limited empirical testing.
- **Counterarguments:** Federalists argue functionalism fragments authority; realists stress power politics.
- **Relevance to Project:** Foundational for classical functionalism; baseline for comparing Chinese functional cooperation.
- **Page References:** Pending full-text review.
- **Status:** METADATA VERIFIED (full text not yet reviewed).

## SRC-0002 — Mitrany, David (1948). The Functional Approach to World Organization
- **Citation:** Mitrany, D. (1948). The functional approach to world organization. *International Affairs*, 24(3), 350-363.
- **Central Argument:** Functional arrangements mitigate equal-sovereignty problems by pooling only necessary sovereignty for joint tasks.
- **Theoretical Contribution:** Clarifies functional "neutrality" and technical self-determination.
- **Limitations:** Assumes technical tasks can be separated from politics.
- **Relevance to Project:** Core classical functionalist concepts.
- **Status:** METADATA VERIFIED.

## SRC-0003 — Haas, Ernst B. (1958). The Uniting of Europe
- **Citation:** Haas, E. B. (1958). *The uniting of Europe: Political, social, and economic forces, 1950-1957*. Stanford University Press.
- **Central Argument:** European integration proceeds via spillover from sectoral cooperation to political integration.
- **Theoretical Contribution:** Neofunctionalism; spillover concept.
- **Limitations:** Over-predicted automatic integration; struggled with 1960s-70s stagnation.
- **Relevance to Project:** Key comparative model; limitations inform Chinese case.
- **Status:** METADATA VERIFIED.

## SRC-0006 — He, Kai (2015). Revisiting the Debate on Constructing a Theory of International Relations with Chinese Characteristics
- **Citation:** He, K. (2015). Revisiting the debate on constructing a theory of international relations with Chinese characteristics. *The China Quarterly*, 222, 432-454.
- **Central Argument:** Chinese IR theory debate reflects national identity and global status quest, with two functional dimensions (academic and policy).
- **Theoretical Contribution:** Differentiates academic meta-theory from policy-oriented concept production.
- **Limitations:** Focuses on discourse, not empirical foreign policy.
- **Relevance to Project:** Shows "Chinese Functionalism" is not an established concept; warns against conflating policy framing with theory.
- **Status:** METADATA VERIFIED.
